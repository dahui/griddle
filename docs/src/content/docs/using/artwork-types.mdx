Steam shows a game through five separate images. Griddle gives each one a tab.

| Tab | Where you see it | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Capsule** | The library grid — the tall tile you scroll past | Portrait, 600×900 |
| **Wide Capsule** | The horizontal shelves: recent games, collections, search | Landscape, 920×430 |
| **Hero** | The wide banner across the top of a game's page | Very wide, 1920×620 |
| **Logo** | The game's name, laid over the hero banner | Transparent PNG |
| **Icon** | Small icon in lists, the taskbar, and desktop shortcuts | Square |

A game can have any combination of them. Changing one never affects the others.

The **Current** tab shows all of them at once, with anything you have not customised labelled
*Steam default*.

## Capsule and Wide Capsule come from the same place

Both are "grids" on SteamGridDB, separated only by shape. That matters when you use
[filters](/griddle/using/filters/): a size that makes sense for one is meaningless for the other,
and Griddle narrows your filter set to whatever the current tab can actually use.

## Logos have a position

A logo is drawn on top of the hero banner, so Steam also stores *where*. When you apply a logo to
a game that has never had one, Griddle writes a sensible default position alongside it — bottom
left, at half size. Without that, some games render no logo at all.

To change it, go to the **Current** tab, right-click the logo and choose **Adjust position…**.
That opens a preview of your logo over your hero banner, with:

- **Anchor** — one of Steam's five positions. There is no bottom-right or top-right; Steam does
  not have them.
- **Width** and **Height** — the logo's size as a percentage of the banner.

Nothing is applied until you press **Save**, so you can try anchors freely. **Reset to default**
puts it back to bottom left at half size. If Steam is running the change appears immediately;
otherwise Griddle writes the position to disk and tells you a restart is needed.

## Icons always need a Steam restart

Icons are the one slot Steam cannot change while it is running — every other kind of artwork
appears instantly, and an icon never does. That is not a limitation of Griddle; the same is true
of the Decky plugin and every other tool.

Apply an icon like any other artwork. Griddle writes it and tells you to restart Steam, and it is
there when Steam comes back. Non-Steam shortcuts need one extra step behind the scenes, which
Griddle handles without closing anything — see
[Non-Steam shortcuts](/griddle/notes/non-steam-shortcuts/).

## Animated artwork

SteamGridDB has animated capsules and heroes, and they work — Steam plays them in both the desktop
library and Big Picture. Griddle marks them in the browser so you can tell before you apply one.

## Tile size

**Size −/+** at the top right, opposite the Library and Settings tabs, makes the tiles bigger or
smaller, and the grid re-flows to fit.

It is on every grid of artwork — each of the five browsing tabs, the **Current** overview, and
your [library list](/griddle/using/finding-your-games/). Each remembers its own setting, so heroes
can stay large while capsules stay dense, and the choice is kept between sessions.

Two deliberate details: it is a pair of buttons rather than a slider, because a slider cannot be
driven with a controller — left and right are how you move between things. And there is no
percentage readout, because a number there would have no meaning outside the control; the grid
resizing under your click is the feedback.

## Looking closer before you apply

A grid of thumbnails is not much to choose from when two capsules look alike. **Right-click any
result** — or press the menu button on a controller — to see it at full size along with:

- who made it, and how it has been voted on
- its exact dimensions and file format
- its style, language, and any notes the author left
- content flags, if it carries any

**View on SteamGridDB** opens the artwork's own page in your browser, which is where to go to
upvote it, report it, or find more by the same author.

Opening the details never applies anything — there is an **Apply** button in there when you have
decided.
